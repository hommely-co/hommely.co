The art of a slow Canadian winter

Five months of cold is a long time to wait for spring. Here's how to stop waiting and start enjoying it.

By the Hommely editors · 4 min read · Updated October 13, 2026

Every November, a lot of us make the same quiet plan: get through it. Keep the head down, count the weeks, survive until the first warm day in April. It's an understandable plan. It's also a terrible way to spend almost half the year.

The Danes have a word for the alternative, and Canadians have been borrowing it for years: hygge, roughly the feeling of being warm, safe and content while the weather does its worst outside. You don't need a Scandinavian cabin for it. You need light, warmth, a few small rituals and a reason to go outside.

1. Fix the light first

By the solstice, much of southern Ontario gets around nine hours of daylight, and most of it happens while we're at work. The single biggest change you can make at home is to stop relying on one bright overhead light. Use three low, warm lights at different heights instead: a lamp on a side table, something glowing on a shelf, a candle (or a flameless one) on the table.

Winter isn't something to get through. It's a season with its own pleasures, if you set the house up for them.

2. Make warmth a ritual

Warmth isn't only the thermostat. It's the blanket that lives on the sofa all season, the slippers by the door, the hot drink that's still hot twenty minutes later. Pick one small ritual and protect it: tea at four, soup on Sundays, twenty minutes of reading under a throw before bed.

3. Go outside on purpose

This is the one people skip. A twenty-minute walk at lunch, while the sun is actually up, does more for a winter mood than any purchase. Dress for it properly (warm boots, a real hat, mittens rather than gloves) and it stops being an ordeal. Skating, a snowy trail, a hot chocolate on a park bench: these are the things you'll actually remember about the season.

4. Cook slow food

Winter is the best cooking season of the year. Braises, stews, roasted vegetables, a pot of chili that makes the whole house smell good: all of them reward time rather than effort. Make double, freeze half, and February-you will be grateful.

5. Plan small things to look forward to

The long stretch after the holidays is the hardest part. Put small things on the calendar now: a games night in January, a Family Day plan, a weekend trip in March. Anticipation is half the pleasure, and it makes the dark weeks feel shorter.

The short version

  • Three warm lamps instead of one ceiling light.
  • A gentle wake-up light for dark mornings.
  • One daily warm ritual you protect.
  • Twenty minutes outside while the sun is up.
  • Something small on the calendar every few weeks.
