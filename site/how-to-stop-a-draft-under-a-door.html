How to stop a draft under a door

Feel cold air on your ankles near the door? You are not imagining it. Hydro One says air leakage accounts for 25 to 40 per cent of the average home's heat loss in winter, and the gap under a door is one of the easiest leaks to fix. Here is how to do it in about ten minutes.

Step 1: Find the draft

On a cold, windy day, hold the back of your hand or a strip of tissue near the bottom of the closed door. If it moves or you feel cold, you have a draft. At night, shine a flashlight from one side while someone checks for light on the other side: light means air.

Step 2: Measure the gap

Close the door and measure the gap at the bottom and the door's width and thickness. A gap under about 1 cm is easy to seal with a sweep; a bigger gap or a worn threshold may need a hardware fix (Step 4).

Drafts around the sides too? Hydro One notes that a properly fitted door should close firmly without air leaking at the sides, top or bottom, and that rubber weatherstripping for the frame costs only a few dollars.

Step 4: When it is the door, not the gap

Many exterior doors have an adjustable threshold: look for screws in the threshold and raise it slightly until the door just touches. If the door is warped, rattles when closed or lets in light around the frame, it may be time for a new door or a professional fix. Questions people ask

What is the fastest way to stop a draft under a door?

Slide a two-sided draft stopper onto the bottom of the door. It takes a minute, needs no tools and moves with the door.

How do renters stop a door draft without damage?

Use a slide-on draft stopper instead of adhesive sweeps or screws. It comes off without marks when you move.

How much heat do drafts waste?

Hydro One says air leakage is 25 to 40 per cent of the average home's heat loss in winter.

Do door draft stoppers work on carpet?

Slide-on foam stoppers work on low carpet. On thick carpet, measure first, as the door may drag.
